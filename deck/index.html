One orbit later the satellite arrives a few milliseconds late, which at 7.6 km/s is tens of meters along-track: about 4,000 times more than the same burn spent pushing sideways in the last seconds. The prograde burn raises the orbit, so the satellite lags behind where it would have been.

Early maneuvers work by changing the orbital period, not by pushing the satellite aside. The along-track offset grows by about 3πΔa every orbit.

This is why lead time matters so much in the maneuver trade space later: the same Δv buys more separation the earlier it is applied.

Avoiding a collision in space requires advance warning, which requires tracking objects in orbit and predicting close approaches before they happen.

This is the key difference with terrestrial collision avoidance. The problem is global and predictive, not local and reactive.

Sensing from orbit can help, but only at enormous scale: SpaceX's Stargaze uses the star trackers on thousands of Starlink satellites.

Stargaze: SpaceX's SSA system using Starlink star trackers, opened to outside operators in August 2026. It does not see small debris and does not replace ground radar.

Multi-hypothesis tracking for track-to-object association

  1. Generate hypotheses. The new track could belong to any object it plausibly matches (gating), be a new object, or be a false alarm.
  2. Score them. How well the track fits each object's predicted orbit, given its uncertainty, plus prior rates of new objects and false alarms.
  3. Extend. When the next track arrives, branch every surviving hypothesis again.
  4. Prune and commit. Drop unlikely branches. Commit to an association only once one hypothesis clearly wins.
Track-to-object association is combinatorial: every new track could match several objects. MHT keeps a tree of association hypotheses, scores each with the measurement likelihood against each object's predicted state and covariance plus priors for new objects and false alarms, and prunes as data arrive (N-scan, keep the K best). Leftover tracks that match nothing become uncorrelated tracks (UCTs); objects that stop matching become lost objects.

Developed for NORAD in the 1960s, when numerically integrating the whole catalog was too expensive: mean elements fitted for the semi-analytic SGP4 propagator, which includes a drag term.

Screening filters: apsis, path and phase

Hoots, Crawford & Roehrich 1984 · scripts/screening.py

Apsis: altitude ranges must overlap. Path: the orbits must come close somewhere (minimum orbit intersection distance). Phase: both objects must be near that point at the same time. Each is conservative: it can keep pairs that won't conjunct but never drops a real one. Vedant (in the room?) showed object-specific uncertainty-aware apsis thresholds remove 71% of all-vs-all pairs. Here: ISS vs 32,877 objects, 7 days in 30-min windows (11.0M object-windows). Apsis keeps 29%, path 19%, phase 0.2%; fine SGP4 screening of the 25k survivors finds 7 candidate events, and a brute-force run confirms none were missed (7/7, and 316/316 approaches within 35 km). Per object the funnel looks weak (13.5k, 10.8k, 7.3k objects pass in some window): over a week, nodal drift means most orbits at ISS altitude intersect somewhere. These are SGP4 screening hits, not risk.

Monte Carlo probability of collision

CALIPSO vs. Cosmos 2251 debris (NASA CARA test case) · scripts/pc.py

Each sample draws both objects' states from their estimates and covariances, propagates them to closest approach (here: straight-line relative motion near TCA, the same assumption as 2D Pc), and counts a hit when the miss distance is inside the combined hard-body radius. Pc is hits over samples. Click: the running estimate for this conjunction as samples accumulate, with its 95% interval, converging on the Foster value (dashed). After 1,000 samples there is one hit; it takes millions of samples to pin Pc down to a few percent.

How many samples are needed?

Interactive: drag the slider to set the true Pc, click a confidence level and a target relative error. Relative standard error of a binomial estimate: sqrt((1-p)/(N p)). For 10% error at 95% confidence, N ~ 384 (1-p)/p. Each sample needs two high-fidelity propagations to TCA.

Foster & Estes 1992, NASA JSC-25898; NASA CARA Pc2D_Foster · scripts/pc.py

Fast encounters (LEO, tens of seconds or less in the uncertainty region) make the straight-line, constant-covariance assumptions good. The integral is over the Gaussian of the relative position in the encounter plane, centered on the predicted miss, evaluated over a disk of the combined radius centered on the secondary. Slow encounters (GEO, formation flying) break the assumptions; that is where 3D Pc or Monte Carlo is used.

Hands up

\(P_c = 10^{-3}\), three days before closest approach. Do you maneuver?

Now · Wait for the next update · No

Take a count for each option. There is no single right answer, which is the point.

\(P_c\) changes as tracking updates arrive before closest approach

scripts/pc_evolution.py

Simulation: covariance growth fitted to real data (Starlink operator ephemerides for the primary, 53 NASA CARA test CDMs for the secondary), estimate errors drawn from each update's covariance. Of truly safe 500 m passes, 38% crossed 1 in 10,000 at some update a day or more out; none were still above it at 6 h. Of true 50 m threats, 99% are above it at 6 h. Early on, you can't tell which is which. Dilution: for this geometry Pc peaks (2e-3) when the covariance is about 0.74 times its CDM size; more uncertainty can make Pc go down, which does not mean safer. Caveat: real CDM updates are correlated; these draws are independent.

Approaches to the maneuver decision

Industry standard: fixed rules (e.g. Pc above 1e-4 at T-24h, maneuver). Better: trade-space tables of maneuver size vs time vs residual risk. Really: a POMDP. Grace Kim's work in SISL: Kim, Eddy, Kochenderfer, "Chance-Constrained Belief-Space Maneuver Planning for Autonomous Collision Avoidance Under Uncertainty," AMOS 2026 (arXiv:2609.13428): in 96 scenarios the planner reached TCA without maneuvering in about 40% of episodes with no terminal-risk violations. Kim, Al-Husseini, Eddy, Kochenderfer, "Semi-Decentralized Multi-Spacecraft Collision Avoidance under Communication Constraints," AAS/AIAA ASC 2026 (arXiv:2607.26570): 28.5% fewer synchronization events than continuous coordination.

Space Force screening: what is screened against what, and when

All HAC (High Accuracy Catalog)

Every object in the SP catalog against every other: near-Earth (period ≤ 225 min) propagated 7 days, deep space 10 days.

Concern list

Re-screens the pairs that All HAC flagged, with TCA ≤ 5 days: \(P_c\) ≥ 10⁻⁷ near-Earth, miss ≤ 5 km deep space.

O/O ephemeris vs HAC

Your uploaded ephemeris against the SP catalog. Operational files: results 8–12 h after the slot. Special (maneuver) files: as soon as possible, results within 4 h.

O/O vs O/O

Operational ephemerides against each other, re-screened every 8 h until less than 8 h (near-Earth) or 12 h (deep space) of the file remains.

19 SDS screens everything at least every 24 h. The catalog is updated and propagated every 8 h (7 days near-Earth, 10 days deep space). One full HAC vs HAC per regime per day, then two concern-list screenings 8 h apart. O/O ephemeris vs HAC: an operational file is screened once, within 8 h of upload; special files are screened as soon as possible and are not screened against other O/O ephemerides. O/O vs O/O: operational files are kept and re-screened every 8 h until less than 8 h (near-Earth) or 12 h (deep space) of ephemeris remains. This is the baseline. If you need faster turnaround, you screen yourself (next slide).

Planning a maneuver: screen, decide, share

Baseline loop: get a CDM, plan candidate maneuvers, submit them to Space-Track as special ephemerides for screening, wait for results (special files within about 4 h, operational 8 to 12 h), decide which to fly, publish the OEM with the burn on Space-Track so others can plan around you, wait for CDMs on the new OEM, and repeat until TCA. Format: CCSDS OEM (optionally with covariance) or Modified ITC, in MEME J2000. SpaceX's space-safety.com returns screening in under a minute. In-house (step 1): with an SSA Sharing Agreement you can pull SP ephemerides for the whole unclassified catalog and screen candidate maneuvers yourself, on demand, in minutes. The SP files have no covariance, so Pc needs your own estimate for the other object or the CDM values. You still publish the chosen ephemeris so 19 SDS and other operators screen against it.

brahe: Earth orbit with a chosen force model

import brahe as bh

epoch = bh.Epoch(2026, 11, 10, 0, 0, 0.0, time_system=bh.TimeSystem.UTC)
x0 = bh.state_koe_to_eci([bh.R_EARTH + 525e3, 0.001, 97.5, 30, 0, 0],
                         bh.AngleFormat.DEGREES)

force = bh.ForceModelConfig(
    gravity=bh.GravityConfiguration.spherical_harmonic(degree=20, order=20),
    drag=bh.DragConfiguration(bh.AtmosphericModel.HARRIS_PRIESTER,
        area=bh.ParameterSource.value(1.2), cd=bh.ParameterSource.value(2.2)),
    third_body=[bh.ThirdBody.SUN, bh.ThirdBody.MOON],
    mass=bh.ParameterSource.value(100.0),
)
prop = bh.NumericalOrbitPropagator(epoch, x0,
    bh.NumericalPropagationConfig.default(), force)
prop.propagate_to(epoch + 3 * 86400.0)
LEO with 20x20 EGM2008 gravity, Harris-Priester drag (1.2 m² area, Cd 2.2), Sun and Moon third-body, 100 kg; propagate 3 days. Runs as shown: pocs/brahe_snippets.py (523 km after 3 days).

brahe: Earth to Mars, with an impulsive burn

bh.load_spice_kernel("de440s")
t0 = bh.Epoch(2028, 12, 10, 0, 0, 0.0, time_system=bh.TimeSystem.TDB)
x0 = bh.spk_state(bh.NAIFId.EARTH, bh.NAIFId.SUN, t0)  # on Earth's orbit

sun = bh.CentralBody.from_naif_id(bh.NAIFId.SUN)
force = bh.ForceModelConfig.for_body(sun,
    gravity=bh.GravityConfiguration.point_mass(),
    third_body=[bh.ThirdBody.JUPITER, bh.ThirdBody.VENUS])

def tmi(epoch, x):  # trans-Mars injection: 3.2 km/s prograde
    x = x.copy()
    x[3:] += 3200.0 * x[3:] / np.linalg.norm(x[3:])
    return x, bh.EventAction.CONTINUE

cruise = bh.NumericalOrbitPropagator(t0, x0,
    bh.NumericalPropagationConfig.default(), force)
cruise.add_event_detector(
    bh.TimeEvent(t0 + 86400.0, "TMI").with_callback(tmi))
cruise.propagate_to(t0 + 204 * 86400.0)  # passes Mars
Sun-centered propagation (central body from NAIF ID 10, the Sun) with Jupiter and Venus perturbations, starting on Earth's orbit from the DE440s ephemeris. A TimeEvent callback applies a 3.2 km/s prograde burn one day in. Departure date and burn size come from a scan of the 2028-29 window (scripts/mars_transfer.py); the spacecraft passes about 0.46 million km from Mars on day 204. The animation replays the daily states. The snippet runs as shown: pocs/brahe_snippets.py.

Radar return falls off as \(1/R^4\); reflected sunlight seen by a telescope falls off as \(1/R^2\)

Radar return power falls as 1/R^4 (out and back). Reflected sunlight seen by a telescope falls as 1/R^2. Going from LEO to GEO range is ~36x farther: radar loses ~1.7 million times the signal, optical about 1,300 times. Hence: radars for LEO, telescopes for GEO. Telescopes need dark skies and a sunlit target.

Maneuver trade space: Δv and burn time vs resulting \(P_c\)

scripts/maneuver.py

Same CALIPSO / Cosmos 2251 debris event (generic 600 kg spacecraft, CDM covariances held fixed). Direction matters too: a small prograde burn first raises Pc (the primary moves toward the secondary), and retrograde is cheaper here (about 3.0, 2.0, 0.6, 0.2 cm/s at 1, 6, 24, 72 h). A tiny along-track burn changes the period, so the along-track shift at TCA grows roughly as 3 Δv t. One day early: about 0.7 cm/s. One hour early: about 7 cm/s, ten times more. At a 2 h lead no burn up to 10 cm/s gets below 1e-4 (see the script's report for why before explaining it): geometry matters, not just lead time.
